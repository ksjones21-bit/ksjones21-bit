# Stakeholder Discovery & Gap Assessment

**Project:** Finance Systems Implementation & Transformation PMO  
**Organization:** Horizon Community Health (Fictional Case Study)  
**Prepared by:** Business Transformation Project Lead  
**Project Stage:** Discovery & Assessment  
**Status:** Draft — Pending Stakeholder Validation

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Horizon Community Health initiated implementation of a cloud-based financial planning and reporting platform approximately nine months ago to improve financial reporting efficiency, departmental budget visibility, and forecasting capabilities.

Despite the investment, Finance continues to rely heavily on manual Excel reporting processes. Stakeholder interviews identified potential weaknesses in data governance, account mapping ownership, reconciliation procedures, implementation coordination, and change management.

The current monthly reporting cycle requires approximately five business days, including an estimated two days dedicated to investigating reporting discrepancies.

A phased 60-day recovery initiative is proposed to establish sustainable reporting controls, improve data reliability, and reduce the monthly reporting cycle from five to three business days.

The proposed approach prioritizes high-impact mapping corrections, a departmental reporting pilot, internal capability development, and executive review at Day 30.

## 2. Business Objectives

Executive discovery identified three original objectives for the planning platform:

1. **Departmental Budget Visibility:** Enable department managers to access reliable financial performance information without depending on repeated manual requests to Finance.
2. **Reporting Efficiency:** Reduce manual report preparation and reconciliation activities.
3. **Forecasting Improvement:** Strengthen the organization's ability to anticipate staffing vacancies, program deficits, and revenue shortfalls.

The CFO identified monthly reporting reliability and efficiency as the immediate priority for the proposed recovery initiative.

Forecasting improvements remain a longer-term objective and are not included as a guaranteed outcome of the initial 60-day scope.

## 4. Current-State Reporting Process

The current monthly reporting workflow includes:

1. Accounting records financial activity and month-end journal entries.
2. The Senior Financial Analyst extracts general ledger actuals, typically on the third business day following month-end.
3. Actuals are uploaded to the planning platform.
4. A separate analyst prepares staffing information from the payroll system.
5. Finance prepares and updates budget-to-actual reports.
6. Analysts investigate differences between planning-system outputs and accounting records.
7. The Finance Director reviews, adjusts, and distributes final reports.

### Current Performance Baseline

| Activity | Estimated Duration |
|---|---:|
| Report preparation and updates | 2 business days |
| Discrepancy investigation | 2 business days |
| Review and distribution | 1 business day |
| **Total reporting cycle** | **5 business days** |

These estimates represent elapsed process time, not necessarily total staff labor hours.

## 5. Preliminary Gap Assessment

### Gap 1 — Undefined Mapping Ownership

**Observation:** The Senior Financial Analyst maintains mappings, but no formally approved owner or decision process has been established.

**Potential impact:** Inconsistent account classifications, unclear accountability, and repeated reporting discrepancies.

**Proposed response:** Establish documented mapping responsibilities, approval authority, and version control.

### Gap 2 — Inconsistent Data Refresh Procedures

**Observation:** Additional journal entries may be posted after actuals are initially uploaded to the planning platform.

**Potential impact:** Differences between accounting and planning reports caused by timing rather than mapping errors.

**Proposed response:** Define reporting cutoff dates, refresh procedures, and reconciliation requirements.

### Gap 3 — Limited Issue Tracking

**Observation:** Reporting discrepancies are not consistently maintained in a centralized register.

**Potential impact:** Recurring problems, duplicated investigation, and limited visibility into resolution progress.

**Proposed response:** Introduce a discrepancy register with issue descriptions, assigned owners, status, and resolution evidence.

### Gap 4 — Insufficient Change Control

**Observation:** Mapping changes have been requested through multiple channels without a consistent approval process.

**Potential impact:** Conflicting instructions, implementation rework, and unintended reporting changes.

**Proposed response:** Implement risk-based approvals, technical impact assessments, testing, and release documentation.

### Gap 5 — Consultant Dependency

**Observation:** Internal staff can upload data but have limited experience maintaining system mapping configurations.

**Potential impact:** Routine changes remain dependent on external consulting support.

**Proposed response:** Use remaining consulting hours for targeted configuration support, documented procedures, and supervised knowledge transfer.

## 6. Preliminary Root-Cause Hypotheses

The following hypotheses require further validation:

- Unclear decision ownership contributes to inconsistent mapping instructions.
- Mapping inconsistencies contribute to departmental reporting discrepancies.
- Uncontrolled data refresh timing contributes to differences between the general ledger and planning platform.
- Limited change documentation allows issues to recur.
- Insufficient internal training increases reliance on the implementation consultant.

These are preliminary hypotheses rather than verified root causes.

## 7. Proposed Improvement Priorities

The initial recovery effort will prioritize:

1. Establishing mapping governance and decision authority.
2. Reconciling high-risk accounts and material reporting differences.
3. Standardizing data refresh and reconciliation procedures.
4. Testing and validating one departmental budget-to-actual report.
5. Training internal staff to maintain approved mapping configurations.
6. Expanding the validated process after the Day 30 executive checkpoint.

Account review will be risk-based, considering financial magnitude, reporting impact, recurring discrepancies, and program or grant-reporting sensitivity.

| Validate account classification | Accounting Manager |

| Resolve significant resource or policy conflicts | CFO |
| Coordinate project execution and reporting | Business Transformation Project Lead |

The governance model requires stakeholder agreement before adoption.

## 9. Proposed Success Measures

| Measure | Baseline | Proposed Target |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly reporting cycle | 5 business days | 3 business days |
| Material unexplained reconciliation differences | To be established | Zero at reporting sign-off |
| Mapping governance | Informal ownership and approvals | Documented, approved process |
| Departmental reporting pilot | Not validated | One reconciled and accepted pilot report by Day 30 |
| Internal mapping maintenance | Consultant-dependent | Trained and authorized internal staff |

The three-day reporting target represents a proposed 40% reduction in elapsed reporting time.

## 10. Preliminary Risks and Constraints

**Reporting accuracy:** Corrected mappings could introduce additional errors if testing and reconciliation are insufficient.

**Technical dependencies:** Changes may affect historical data, integrations, or downstream reporting.

**Resource availability:** Analyst capacity is tentatively estimated at 10 hours per week and may be constrained during budget preparation.

**Consulting capacity:** Approximately 20 consulting hours remain under the existing agreement.

**Historical reporting:** Material changes may affect previously distributed management reports and require documented impact assessment and approval.

## 11. Recommended Next Steps

The Business Transformation Project Lead recommends:

1. Validate discovery findings with Finance, Accounting, and IT.
2. Confirm resource commitments and decision authority.
3. Obtain the approved reporting baseline and current mapping inventory.
4. Prioritize discrepancies using financial and operational risk.
5. Approve a phased 60-day recovery charter.
6. Complete a controlled departmental reporting pilot by Day 30.
7. Present pilot results and remaining risks for an executive go/no-go decision before expansion.
